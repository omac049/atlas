Where Kalshi and Polymarket stand legally (as of September 2026)

A factual tracker of public rulings and filings, not legal advice. This area is changing month to month; check the sources for the current state.

The federal picture

Kalshi is a CFTC-regulated exchange. Polymarket US operates a separately regulated US venue; Polymarket's original global platform is not available to US accounts. Federal courts have generally treated Kalshi's event contracts as swaps under the Commodity Exchange Act, which would preempt state gambling law — a US appellate court allowed Kalshi to keep offering sports contracts in New Jersey while litigation continues.

Where states have pushed back on sports contracts

StateWhat has happened
New YorkThe Attorney General filed a civil suit calling Kalshi's sports contracts illegal gambling.
MassachusettsA Superior Court judge issued a preliminary injunction barring in-state sports contracts without a gaming license (January 2026).
NevadaA temporary restraining order covered sports, election, and entertainment contracts; Kalshi removed those categories for Nevada users.
New JerseyA federal appellate court held the state cannot enforce its sports-betting laws against Kalshi while the case proceeds.

The pattern so far: federal courts lean toward CFTC jurisdiction; several state courts and regulators treat sports contracts as gambling. Economic and political contracts have drawn less state action than sports contracts. Your own state's status can change with a single ruling.
