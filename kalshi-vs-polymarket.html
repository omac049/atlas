Kalshi vs Polymarket: the factual comparison

Kalshi is a US-regulated exchange. Polymarket runs two venues: a US one and the original global platform, which US accounts cannot use. They list many of the same events, charge fees on a similar curve, and settle by different rulebooks. This page puts what each venue publishes next to each other — regulation, who can use it, funding, fees, settlement, disputes, taxes — with a source on every row and no opinion on which is "better".

Trading fees (published formulas)
[1] [2] [3] [4] [5]
Fee schedule 'Last updated and effective: July 7, 2026': taker fee = round up(M × 0.07 × C × P × (1−P)) with M 'default is 1 unless otherwise indicated'; maker fee = round up(M × 0.0175 × C × P × (1−P)) with M 'default is 0 unless otherwise indicated' (so 0.0175 is exactly 25% of 0.07, but it applies only to series whose maker multiplier is set to 1). The Non-Standard Fees table lists many series at maker 1 / taker 1 (e.g., NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL, NCAA games and championships, CPI, Fed decisions, jobs, GDP, Emmys, PGA, tennis) and some at 0 / 0 (e.g., BTC and ETH year-end price ranges, several geopolitical questions). Maximum general taker fee is $1.75 per 100 contracts at $0.50; there is no settlement fee and no membership fee. Perpetual futures use separate volume-tiered bps schedules (taker 12.0 bps down to 2.6 bps; maker 5.0 bps down to 0.6 bps).Fee schedule 'Effective exchange-wide from 12 AM ET, Wednesday July 1, 2026': Fee = Θ × C × p × (1−p), where C is contracts and p is the decimal price ($0.01–$0.99); taker Θ = 0.06 (maximum $1.50 per 100-contract lot at p = $0.50); maker rebate Θ = −0.0125 (maximum $0.31 rebate per 100 contracts). Volume-based taker rebates apply above $250,000 of prior-month taker volume (10%), $1,000,000 (25%) and $10,000,000 (50%). Fees and rebates are rounded to the nearest $0.01 using banker's rounding.Only takers pay: fee = C × feeRate × p × (1−p), where the rate depends on category — Crypto 0.07 (peak $1.75 per 100 shares); Sports, Economics, Culture, Weather and Other 0.05 (peak $1.25); Finance, Politics, Mentions and Tech 0.04 (peak $1.00); Geopolitics 0 (fee-free). 'Makers are never charged fees'; the Maker Rebates Program redistributes a share of taker fees daily in pUSD (Crypto 20%, Sports 15%, other fee-enabled categories 25%; $1 pUSD minimum payout).
Note: The brief's premise that Kalshi applies a 'higher multiplier for some categories' is not supported by the July 7, 2026 schedule we loaded: every listed series carries a multiplier of 1 or 0 (a combo series is listed separately). Kalshi's help center says 'Trading fees are not charged for orders... left as resting orders... unless they are included in our Maker Fees section', which is consistent with the schedule's maker default of 0.

Contract structure, price range, minimum size, tick size
[1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8] [9]
Binary YES/NO event contracts that pay a Settlement Value (e.g., $1.00) to the winning side; the KalshiEX Rulebook (v1.15, January 17, 2025) states 'The minimum unit of trading is one Contract' and prices are 'quoted in U.S. Dollars and cents per Contract' with a minimum quote increment of $0.01. Kalshi's API documentation (last updated August 20, 2026) now defines per-market price structures whose center-range ticks are $0.01, $0.005, $0.002, $0.001 or $0.0001, with 'tapered' structures applying a finer tick near the edges of the price range (roughly below $0.10 and above $0.90).Contracts are 'priced between $0 and $1'; winning contracts 'settle at $1.00', losing at '$0.00' (some markets have other predefined settlement terms). Docs: 'All trades are executed in whole event contracts' and 'Polymarket US does not set trading size limits'; the Rulebook (Aug 28, 2026) Rule 10.1 states 'Contracts may be traded in fractional units' and 'The minimum quote increment for each Contract is $0.001 per Contract unless otherwise specified'. How markets are settled and who decides
[1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]
'A market settles when the official outcome is confirmed and our markets team finalizes the result'; Kalshi relies on 'a source agency (for example, official league statistics, government releases, or other event authorities)' named in each market's rules, and most markets settle 'within approximately 3 hours' of the outcome becoming known. Under Rulebook Rule 6.3, if the outcome cannot be determined Kalshi may settle at the last traded price or refer the matter to its Outcome Review Committee, a committee of the board.'The Exchange determines the result using publicly verifiable information' from the sources listed in each market's rules — government data, results 'published by the governing league or competition organizer', or named news outlets — and 'Unlisted sources have no effect on market resolution'. 'Polymarket Clearing processes the settlement and updates balances automatically' at $1.00/$0.00; 'All resolutions are final in accordance with the Polymarket US Exchange Rulebook'. No oracle is used.'Markets are resolved by the UMA Optimistic Oracle': anyone may propose an outcome by posting a bond ('typically $750 pUSD'); the proposal enters a 2-hour challenge period; if undisputed it finalizes and winning shares pay $1. If disputed, a second proposal round follows, and a second dispute escalates to UMA's Data Verification Mechanism where UMA token holders vote (roughly 24–48 hours of debate plus about 48 hours of voting); the losing party's bond is split with the winner.

[1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8]

Questions people ask

Is Polymarket legal in the US?

There are two separate platforms. polymarket.com blocks trading in the United States and lists the US among its fully blocked countries; Polymarket US (QCX LLC d/b/a Polymarket US) is a CFTC-designated contract market that serves US residents through its own app and separate accounts.

Can I use Polymarket in the US?

US users can use the Polymarket US app (iOS and Google Play, KYC required, 18+ and age of majority in your state) but 'cannot deposit funds or place trades on the website'; the app-store listings note availability restrictions (Nevada is named as unavailable on the iOS listing). Polymarket US accounts are 'entirely separate' from polymarket.com accounts.

Is Kalshi regulated?

Yes. KalshiEX LLC received a CFTC Order of Designation as a designated contract market on November 4, 2020, and its affiliate Kalshi Klear LLC is a CFTC-registered derivatives clearing organization.

Which has lower fees, Kalshi or Polymarket?

Published taker formulas at a $0.50 price: Kalshi 0.07 × P × (1−P) = $1.75 per 100 contracts (July 7, 2026 schedule); Polymarket US 0.06 × p × (1−p) = $1.50 per 100 contracts plus a maker rebate of up to $0.31 per 100 (July 1, 2026); polymarket.com charges takers 0.04–0.07 depending on category ($1.00–$1.75 per 100 shares) and 0 in Geopolitics, with makers paying nothing. Which is cheapest for a given trade depends on price, category and whether the order is a maker or taker.

Does Kalshi send a 1099?

Kalshi issues Form 1099-INT for interest, 1099-MISC for credits/rewards, 1099-B for crypto-transfer proceeds and 1099-DA for digital-asset reporting via ZeroHash when IRS thresholds are met; trading profit and loss is provided as a FIFO PnL statement rather than on a 1099.

Does Polymarket send a 1099?

Polymarket publishes no tax-form page for either platform. A CPA source states the on-chain platform 'does not issue Form 1099-B, 1099-DA, or W-2G'; Polymarket US's documentation index has no tax-documents page.

Are Kalshi and Polymarket the same markets?

No. They are separate exchanges with separate contracts, rules and settlement processes: Kalshi's markets team settles each contract from the source agency named in its rules, Polymarket US's exchange settles from sources listed in each market's rules and clears through Polymarket Clearing, and polymarket.com resolves through the UMA Optimistic Oracle with bonded proposals and a 2-hour challenge period. Prices on one venue do not settle contracts on another.

Can I trade Kalshi markets through my broker?

Kalshi contracts are offered through Robinhood Derivatives, LLC ('through KalshiEX LLC'), Webull Financial LLC ('provided by Kalshi') and Coinbase Financial Markets ('operated by Kalshi'); Coinbase's product is limited to US residents excluding Nevada. Users trading via a futures commission merchant may be charged fees that differ from Kalshi's own schedule.
